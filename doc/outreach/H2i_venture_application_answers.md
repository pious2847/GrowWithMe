### Project Description (171 words)

Problem: In Northern Ghana, long distances to facilities, poor connectivity, low literacy and referrals that quietly die between CHPS compounds and hospitals drive preventable maternal and child deaths. Ghana's maternal mortality ratio is 234 per 100,000 live births, with northern regions above average; about 30% of children under five are stunted and roughly three in four households face food insecurity.

Solution: GrowWithMe is a local-first Android app that works fully offline. It combines IMCI-aligned child and pregnancy triage with adaptive questions and deterministic danger rules that always override the AI; a care calendar synced to Ghana's EPI schedule with spoken reminders; WHO growth monitoring; and 'Nana', a voice-first AI educator. One tap generates a PDF patient report SMSed to the nearest CHPS compound, volunteer responders and the mother's hospital, with every alert tracked to closure on a facility dashboard.

Market: Our initial market is the roughly 2.5 million people of Northern Region and adjacent northern regions, scaling to Ghana's under-five and maternal population and other low-connectivity West African settings.

## Venture Readiness Assessment

### 1. Problem (145 words)

Northern Ghana carries some of the country's worst maternal and child health outcomes. Ghana's maternal mortality ratio is 234 deaths per 100,000 live births, with northern regions historically above the national average; around 30% of children under five are stunted, roughly three in four households face food insecurity, and only about one child in four receives a minimum acceptable diet. The need has been defined to us by local mothers, CHPS nurses and community health volunteers we have engaged: long distances to facilities, poor connectivity, low literacy and referrals that quietly die between CHPS compounds and hospitals. Working mothers miss immunisation, growth monitoring and ANC/PNC visits; caregivers cannot judge symptom severity; and facilities have no visibility of which families in their catchment are overdue or recovering. Existing digital-health tools fail because they assume a smartphone, data credit or literacy families do not have.

### 2. Market (134 words)

Ghana has a population of about 34 million, with roughly four million children under five and maternal-health need concentrated in the Northern, Savannah and North East regions. Our beachhead is Northern Region's maternal and child population - an estimated hundreds of thousands of mothers and children under five across CHPS catchment areas - expanding to Ghana's national under-five cohort and, longer term, low-connectivity West African markets. We reach them through a consumer Android app (free at point of use), SMS/USSD for feature phones, and institutions that procure for scale: Ghana Health Service, district health directorates, NGOs and foundations. Because the product is offline-first and voice-first, our serviceable market includes low-literacy and no-data households excluded by conventional apps. Initial pilot target: 1,500-2,000 mother-child registrations in one district, with a national scale-up pathway through GHS.

### 3. Team (132 words)

Abdul Hafis Mohammed, Founder and CEO, leads product, engineering and partnerships. He built the entire stack in-house: the offline-first Flutter app, Node/Express/MongoDB backend, Arkesel SMS referral gateway, hybrid AI integration (NVIDIA NIM Llama 3.1, ElevenLabs, on-device fallback) and facility dashboard API. TOVO LABs is a Ghanaian software engineering and product studio; the founding team works full-time without salary and is founder-funded. We do not yet have a formal scientific or business advisory board; we are in active conversations with the Ghana Health Service Country Innovation Platform for clinical review and with the University for Development Studies, Tamale, for joint evaluation. We are recruiting a CHPS nurse and a maternal-health clinician as clinical advisers for the supervised pilot, and would value H2i introductions to experienced health-tech mentors and board members.

### 4. Technology / Solution (144 words)

GrowWithMe is a working Android product, not a concept. Caregivers get IMCI-aligned triage for children and pregnancy: adaptive questions, deterministic danger rules that always override the AI, and full offline operation. A WHO growth-chart module flags weight below minus two standard deviations. An EPI-synced care calendar schedules immunisations, Vitamin A, deworming, ANC/PNC and growth checks with spoken reminders. 'Nana', a voice-first AI educator with a Ghanaian-elder persona, offers chat, spoken briefings and agentic actions with confirmation; core commands work offline via a local intent parser. One tap generates a PDF patient report SMSed to the nearest geo-routed volunteer, CHPS compound and registered hospital, with every alert tracked to closure on a facility dashboard. No patents filed; we intend to open-source components as funders require. Regulatory plan: Ghana Data Protection Act compliance and GHS protocol alignment, with content clinically reviewed before the supervised pilot.

### 5. Customer / Revenue Model (126 words)

Customers are, first, mothers and children under five in Northern Ghana, reached free at point of use; and, second, institutions that fund and deploy at scale - Ghana Health Service, district health directorates, NGOs and foundations. The model is pre-revenue today (founded 2026). We plan a hybrid: institutional contracts and per-facility/per-CHW licences for the dashboard and referral infrastructure, with donor and CSR subsidy keeping the caregiver app free; later, optional premium services and telecom zero-rating partnerships. We are in discussions with MTN Ghana Foundation about airtime zero-rating, which would remove the last data barrier for SMS alerts. Revenue is not the near-term driver - a supervised district pilot, measured outcomes and a GHS scale-up agreement are - but the institutional procurement route gives a credible path to sustainability.

### 6. Go To Market (133 words)

Pilot first, then scale through Ghana's public health structure. Months 1-3: complete TOVO LABs' company registration and sign a supervised-pilot agreement with GHS or the Northern Region Health Directorate, finalising consent and data-protection procedures. Months 4-6: train about 40 community health workers and launch in one Northern Region district across roughly five CHPS facilities, targeting 1,500-2,000 mother-child registrations. Months 7-12: operate the pilot, timestamping every urgent alert from danger sign to facility arrival; iterate from CHW and mother feedback; publish open-source components; and measure EPI completion and ANC/PNC attendance against the district baseline. Channels: the GHS CHPS network, district directorates, community partners such as RAINS and Moving Health Foundation, and the HENT/Africa Health Collaborative ecosystem. Customer acquisition cost should be low because CHWs and facility staff do the onboarding.

### 7. Competition (132 words)

Most maternal-health apps in Ghana are content or reminder tools that require a smartphone, data credit and literacy, and stop at generic advice. Global CHW platforms such as CommCare/Dimagi and Medic's Community Health Toolkit are powerful but clinician-facing, not caregiver-facing, and do not run a family-to-facility referral loop offline. Facility dashboards such as DHIMS2 report upwards but do not reach households. AI symptom checkers are text-heavy, cloud-dependent and not aligned to Ghana's IMCI/EPI protocols. GrowWithMe differs by being built for this context from day one: fully offline core, voice-first for low literacy, deterministic IMCI-aligned rules that bound the AI, and a closed SMS referral loop tracked to facility arrival with a facility dashboard. That combination - not any single feature - is our defensible advantage, along with being locally built and GHS-aligned.

### 8. Value Proposition (142 words)

Care that works where the network doesn't. Three differentiators map directly to the problem. First, offline-first: triage, calendar, growth monitoring and core Nana commands run with data switched off, so connectivity and data credit stop being barriers. Second, bounded AI: a deterministic IMCI-aligned rules engine owns every verdict and can only raise, never lower, an AI suggestion - making decisions auditable and defensible to health authorities, unlike LLM-first products. Third, closed referral loop: most tools stop at 'alert sent'; GrowWithMe tracks each urgent case from danger sign to confirmed facility arrival, sends a structured PDF report, and nudges when visits are missed or growth falters. Voice-first and consent-first design make it usable and trustworthy for low-literacy mothers. For families the value is timely care and a real response when it matters; for facilities it is visibility into a catchment they cannot currently see.


### 9. Financials (147 words)

Sales and profit to date: $0 - GrowWithMe was founded in 2026 and is pre-revenue, with no external investment, grants or loans. Founder contributions include well over a year of unpaid engineering across mobile, backend, AI and SMS integration, personal laptops and Android test devices, internet and cloud costs, SMS gateway test credits, and a self-created 55,000-example training dataset for the on-device intent model. We are now raising about $100,000 to convert the prototype into field evidence: pilot operations, CHW devices, SMS and data costs, clinical-content review and independent evaluation. The founding team works full-time without salary, extending runway through sweat equity. Projections: no product revenue during the pilot; thereafter institutional procurement and programme funding become realistic once measured EPI/ANC outcomes and a GHS scale-up pathway exist. We are not optimising for an early exit; the goal is sustainable scale-up with Ghana's public health system.

### 10. Milestones (129 words)

Shipped and verified end-to-end: phone/OTP auth with consent-first onboarding; offline IMCI-aligned child and pregnancy triage with adaptive questions; urgent geo-routed volunteer and facility alerts with PDF patient report and tracked SMS; EPI-synced care calendar; pregnancy journey and newborn handoff; WHO growth monitoring; nutrition engine and Nana's Kitchen meal plans; 'Nana' AI educator in chat and voice with offline fallback; facility dashboard API and Care Signals scan. Next 12 months: register TOVO LABs; sign a GHS supervised-pilot agreement and complete data-protection review; train about 40 CHWs and launch in one Northern Region district across roughly five CHPS facilities, targeting 1,500-2,000 registrations; timestamp every urgent alert to facility arrival; demonstrate measurable EPI/ANC/PNC improvement; publish open-source components; deliver the pilot report and multi-district scale-up plan with GHS.
