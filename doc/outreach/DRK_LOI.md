## Letter of interest (~450 words)

Dear DRK Foundation team,

I'm Abdul Hafis Mohammed, founder of TOVO LABs, a Ghanaian software engineering studio. I'm writing about GrowWithMe, our offline-first care platform for mothers and children under five in Northern Ghana — a working product today, seeking catalytic support to become a measured, scalable intervention.

The problem is stark and structural. Northern Ghana carries the country's worst maternal and child health outcomes: a national maternal mortality ratio of 234 per 100,000 live births, concentrated in the North; roughly 30% of northern children stunted; and a referral system in which mothers quietly die between the village and the hospital. Existing digital-health tools fail this population because they assume smartphone ownership, data credit and literacy that families do not have.

GrowWithMe assumes the opposite. It is an Android platform that works with the network completely off: IMCI-aligned child and pregnancy triage governed by deterministic clinical rules; WHO growth monitoring with automatic malnutrition flags; a care calendar synced to Ghana's immunization schedule with spoken reminders for low-literacy mothers; and "Nana", a voice-first AI health educator whose compact on-device model keeps core commands working in airplane mode. When triage returns urgent, one tap generates a PDF patient report and SMSes it to the nearest clinic, registered volunteer responders and the mother's hospital — and the alert is tracked to confirmed facility arrival. A facility dashboard surfaces missed visits, growth faltering and lost-to-follow-up families before they disappear.

Three things make us a credible bet. First, the product exists: the full stack was built in-house — Flutter offline-first app, backend, SMS gateway, hybrid cloud/on-device AI — and every core flow is verified working, including in airplane mode; the code is public on GitHub. Second, the design is defensible to health authorities: a deterministic rules engine, never the AI, owns every risk verdict. Third, the pathway is real: we are in conversation with the Ghana Health Service's innovation platform for a supervised district pilot, with implementation partners in Northern Ghana, and we are applying to UNICEF's Femtech Ventures call (deadline this week) and MIT Solve's Global Health Challenge.

We are seeking $300,000 over three years to run and evaluate the district pilot (1,500–2,000 mother-child registrations with ~40 community health workers), publish open-source components, and prepare scale-up with the Ghana Health Service into additional districts — converting a working product into the evidence base that unlocks larger institutional funding.

DRK's model — unrestricted, multi-year, early — matches our inflection point exactly. I would welcome a conversation, and I can demo the working product live at any time.

Sincerely,
